# POST 4: Microsoft Copilot — What's Actually Happening in the Real World

## The Story
70% of Fortune 500 companies are now using Copilot. Lumen Technologies claims $50M in annual savings. Accenture deployed it to 200,000 users. But the dirty secret: ROI is almost entirely determined by adoption rate, not the technology.

---

## LinkedIn Post (copy-paste ready)

70% of Fortune 500 companies are now running Microsoft Copilot.

Here's what the results actually look like — not the marketing deck version.

**The wins are real:**
- Lumen Technologies: $50M annual savings from Copilot in sales ops
- Vodafone: 3 hours per week saved per employee (10% of their working week)
- Accenture: 200,000 users, 97% completing routine tasks 15x faster
- Forrester's verified number: 116% ROI over 3 years for enterprises

That's not nothing.

**But here's what nobody's putting in the press release:**

Organisations hitting 70%+ weekly active usage get 3-4x the ROI of those stuck at 30%.

Same tool. Same price. Completely different outcome.

The technology isn't the variable. Adoption is.

And adoption isn't a training issue. It's a change management issue. It's a leadership issue. It's a "does my manager actually use this thing?" issue.

Every Copilot rollout I've seen that struggled had the same problem: they bought the licences and assumed behaviour would follow.

It doesn't.

The companies winning with Copilot treated it like a culture change, not a software deployment.

That's the whole game.
