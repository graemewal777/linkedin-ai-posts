# Gartner Says 40% of Agentic AI Initiatives Will Be Cancelled by 2027

## Story/Angle
Gartner's fresh projection that 40%+ of agentic AI initiatives will be cancelled before end of 2027 — due to escalating costs and unclear business value — is the most important warning for enterprise transformation leaders this week. Picked because it's a concrete forward-looking stat from a credible source, directly relevant to the wave of agent deployments underway.

## LinkedIn Post

Gartner just published a projection that should make every enterprise AI lead pause:

More than 40% of agentic AI initiatives will be cancelled before the end of 2027.

The stated reasons: escalating costs and business value that nobody can clearly quantify.

I've been in digital transformation long enough to recognise this pattern. It's not the first time we've seen it — we saw it with RPA, with blockchain, with "big data." A wave of investment, a lot of announcements, then a quiet reckoning when the CFO asks what we actually got for it.

What makes this one different is the speed. Agentic AI is being deployed faster than any previous enterprise technology wave. Boards are asking about it. Vendors are pitching it. Budgets are being approved. And a lot of those deployments are happening without clear answers to two basic questions: what problem are we actually solving, and how will we know if it worked?

A 28% success rate on AI infrastructure projects (Gartner's parallel finding from a late September survey) isn't a technology failure. The same survey said one in five projects failed because they were "overly ambitious or poorly scoped." That's a planning failure.

The projects I see working share one thing: they start with a painful, specific process problem — something measurable — and then ask whether AI can help. They don't start with "we need to do something with agents."

The ones heading for cancellation started the other way around.

If you're running an agentic AI programme right now: can you answer, in one sentence, what success looks like by Q2 next year? If not, that's worth sorting out before the budget cycle comes back around.
