# Microsoft Copilot Just Hit 30 Million Paid Seats — Here's What the Numbers Actually Tell Us

## Story/Angle
Microsoft's FY26 Q4 earnings revealed 30M+ paid M365 Copilot seats, with large enterprise deals (50k+ seats) growing 7x year over year and net adds doubling quarter over quarter. This is the clearest signal yet that enterprise AI moved from pilot to procurement — picked because of the specificity of the growth metrics.

## LinkedIn Post

30 million paid seats.

That's where Microsoft 365 Copilot sits after this quarter's earnings. And buried in the same report: enterprise deals of 50,000+ seats grew 7x year over year. Net new seat additions more than doubled quarter over quarter.

I want to sit with those numbers for a second.

A year ago the conversation was "are enterprises actually paying for this or just trialing it?" Apparently a lot of them decided to pay. The 7x growth in large-deal size tells you this isn't mid-market experimentation — it's finance teams signing off on six-figure seat counts. That only happens when someone in the business showed concrete numbers justifying the spend.

What's interesting is the bundling play underneath this. Microsoft is merging Copilot Chat, the Cowork agent platform, and GitHub Copilot into a single super-app. For enterprise buyers that's a simpler conversation: one licence, one renewal, embedded in the tools people already use. Procurement loves simplicity.

But here's the thing I keep reminding clients: 30 million seats isn't 30 million people doing different work. Adoption and genuine workflow redesign are two different things. The seat count tells you companies are paying. It tells you nothing about whether the work is actually changing.

The McKinsey data from the same week is the counterpoint: only 25% of non-high-performers have fundamentally redesigned workflows around AI. Seats without redesign is just an expensive autocomplete subscription.

If you've deployed Copilot at scale — what moved the needle from "people using it occasionally" to "we can't imagine working without it"?
