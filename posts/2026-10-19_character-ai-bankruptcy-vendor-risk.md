# Character.AI Just Filed for Bankruptcy. What Does Your AI Vendor Risk Look Like?

## Story/Angle
Character.AI — which had 20 million monthly users at its peak — filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy this week. State attorneys general moved immediately to protect children's chat histories from the bankruptcy process. It's a dramatic consumer story, but the vendor risk question it raises applies directly to enterprise AI buyers.

## LinkedIn Post

A company with 20 million monthly users just filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy.

Character.AI — the AI companion platform that let users build and chat with custom AI personas — hit the wall this week. And the detail that should make every enterprise AI leader uncomfortable isn't the collapse itself. It's what happened next: state attorneys general are scrambling to get a federal judge to protect children's chat histories before the data gets caught up in the liquidation process.

Think about the data your AI systems hold right now. Customer conversations. Employee interactions. HR queries through an AI assistant. Sensitive workflows running through agents. If your AI vendor collapsed tomorrow — or got acquired under pressure, or faced regulatory action — what would happen to that data?

Character.AI's situation is extreme. But the underlying exposure isn't unique to consumer AI.

Every enterprise running AI on sensitive data has a version of this risk. Most haven't priced it.

Three questions I'd be asking my team this week:
1. Which AI vendors have access to data we'd be embarrassed to lose control of?
2. What do our contracts actually say about data retention and portability on termination?
3. Do we have a formal AI vendor risk framework, or are we just trusting the terms of service?

Rapid growth can mask fundamental fragility. 20 million users didn't protect Character.AI. Governance, safety practices, and business model durability did — and those were all gaps.

What does your AI vendor risk process actually look like? I'd genuinely like to know.
