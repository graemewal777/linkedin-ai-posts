# The AI ROI Gap: Why Your Governance Structure Is Costing You Returns

## Story/Angle
The Wharton–GBK 2026 AI study, released October 13, surveyed enterprise leaders and produced the most actionable ROI data I've seen this year. The gap between "positive ROI" and "strongly positive ROI" comes down to two specific choices — and most companies are making both wrong.

## LinkedIn Post

80% of enterprise leaders say AI is delivering positive returns.

Only 28% say those returns are *strongly* positive.

That gap — between "it's working" and "it's really working" — is the most important number in the Wharton–GBK AI study released last week. And the research is pretty clear on what's driving the difference.

Two things stood out:

**Governance structure matters more than most people admit.** Companies with a dedicated Chief AI Officer reported strong ROI 58% of the time. Companies that tagged AI oversight onto an existing executive? Just 24%. That's a 34-point gap. Not noise — a structural difference.

**What you measure shapes what you get.** Businesses tracking AI against *growth outcomes* reported strong returns 35% of the time. Those focused only on efficiency: 13%.

I see this constantly. A leader deploys AI, cuts costs, reports the savings, and calls it a win — without ever asking whether the business actually grew. The efficiency case is easy to make and easy to defend. The growth case requires more courage.

Here's the uncomfortable question this raises: if you don't have a dedicated AI accountability owner, and you're measuring success mostly in headcount reductions — which 24% are you in?

The companies pulling ahead aren't just deploying more AI. They're governing it properly and measuring it against the right outcomes.
