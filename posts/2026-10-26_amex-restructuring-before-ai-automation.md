# Amex Is Removing Management Layers Before the Automation Arrives

## Story/Angle
American Express announced roughly a 5% global workforce reduction, booking a $336 million restructuring charge. The stated rationale — to flatten management spans of control before AI automation — is unusual in its candour and represents a distinct transformation philosophy: redesign the org structure first, then automate into it. This is directly relevant to enterprise transformation leaders thinking about how to actually capture AI value rather than just deploy AI tools.

## LinkedIn Post

American Express is cutting roughly 5% of its global workforce and booking a $336 million restructuring charge.

The stated reason: to remove management layers before AI automation arrives.

That phrase — "before AI automation" — is doing a lot of work.

Most companies are adding AI on top of existing structure. They buy Copilot licences, spin up chatbots, run pilots. The org chart stays the same. The approval chains stay the same. The bureaucracy stays the same. Then they wonder why the productivity numbers from the vendor don't show up in the P&L.

Amex is doing it the other way around. They're taking the cost and the disruption upfront — removing the layers that AI will eventually make redundant — so they can actually capture the value when automation lands.

This is a different transformation philosophy entirely.

The common approach: automate what you have.
The Amex approach: redesign what you have, then automate into it.

Which is harder? The Amex approach, by a distance. You're making real people redundant based on a future state that hasn't arrived yet. That takes conviction, a clear model of where AI is going, and leadership willing to absorb short-term cost for long-term gain.

But it's also the approach more likely to show up in actual financial results — not just in productivity surveys or hours-saved metrics.

For anyone leading transformation: the question isn't just "where can AI save time?" It's "what do we need to change structurally so those time savings convert to real value?"

Are you redesigning before you automate — or automating what you've already got?
