# Meta Just Handed Enterprise AI Risk Straight Back to You

## Story/Angle
Meta updated its Business AI Terms with an effective date of October 30, 2026, explicitly stating that enterprise customers are "solely responsible" for all risks arising from Meta's AI features — while Meta's own contract language acknowledges those features "may make mistakes." This is directly relevant to any business that has deployed Meta AI tools, and it signals a wider industry trend in enterprise AI contracting.

## LinkedIn Post

Meta just handed enterprise AI risk straight back to you.

From October 30, their revised Business AI Terms state that enterprise customers are "solely responsible" for all risks from Meta's AI features.

The kicker? Meta's own contract acknowledges those features "may make mistakes."

Let me write that out plainly:
— You deploy Meta AI tools in your business.
— The tools make a mistake.
— You bear the legal and operational consequences. Not Meta.

This isn't a surprise to anyone who reads vendor contracts carefully. But Meta making it this explicit, right now, while enterprise AI adoption is accelerating — that's worth paying attention to.

I've been saying for months that procurement and legal teams need to be in AI governance conversations from day one. Most are still an afterthought. The business team picks the tool, the IT team deploys it, and legal finds out six months later.

Three questions every transformation leader should be asking right now:

1. Who reviewed the AI vendor contracts you've already signed?
2. Do your agreements include any meaningful liability protection?
3. If an AI tool gives a customer wrong information — who's on the hook?

Most organisations I talk to haven't worked through the legal exposure of their current AI deployments. They're focused on use cases and adoption rates. That's fine, until something goes wrong.

Digital transformation isn't just about what AI can do. It's about who takes the risk when it doesn't.

Has your legal team been involved in your AI vendor relationships from the start?
