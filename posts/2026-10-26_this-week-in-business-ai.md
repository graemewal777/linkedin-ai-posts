# This Week in Business AI — 26 October 2026

## Story/Angle
Three stories stood out this week: a concrete AI productivity win at Toshiba, a significant liability shift in Meta's enterprise AI contracts (effective October 30), and Goldman Sachs weighing in on when — and why — the real AI job wave hits.

## LinkedIn Post

This week in business AI — here's what stood out:

✅ THE WIN
Toshiba confirmed their Microsoft Copilot rollout to 10,000 employees is saving 5.6 hours per employee per month. Not projected — measured. That's 56,000 hours recovered monthly, handed back to the business. Numbers like that only appear when someone actually checked.

⚠️ THE WARNING
Meta just updated their Business AI Terms. From October 30, enterprise customers are "solely responsible" for all risks from Meta's AI features — even though Meta's own contract admits those features "may make mistakes." You carry the liability for their errors. If you've deployed Meta AI tools in your business, your legal team needs to know this exists.

📈 THE TREND
Goldman Sachs published research this week saying the big AI-driven layoff wave hasn't arrived yet — it's waiting for a recession. Meanwhile, American Express isn't waiting. They're cutting ~5% of staff and booking a $336 million charge specifically to remove management layers before AI automation arrives. That's not a response to AI. That's preparation for it.

---
Two things are becoming clear. The companies capturing real AI value are measuring outcomes in hours, not impressions. And the ones preparing seriously aren't buying more tools — they're restructuring first.
