# Goldman Sachs Says AI Layoffs Are Waiting for a Recession. Gartner Says You'll Regret the Ones You've Already Done.

## Story/Angle
Two reports landed in the same week with conflicting AI workforce messages. Goldman Sachs (Fortune, 30 Oct 2026) says AI could displace 1 in 11 workers but companies are holding off big cuts for the next recession. Gartner (IT Pro, same week) says 30% of workers laid off due to AI will be rehired by 2029 — at far higher cost. Together they tell a coherent and uncomfortable story.

## LinkedIn Post

Goldman Sachs published a warning this week that stopped me mid-scroll.

AI could displace roughly 1 in 11 workers — about 9% of the global workforce. But Goldman's economists think the big wave of AI-triggered layoffs is being held back. Companies are waiting for the next recession, when they'll have political and financial cover to make cuts they're already planning.

That's the cynical read. Here's the counterpoint.

Gartner dropped its own number this week: 30% of workers who've already been laid off due to AI will be rehired by 2029. Not because the AI failed. Because organisations cut too deep, too fast, and didn't understand what those roles actually did.

Rehired — but at significantly higher cost.

I've watched this happen with outsourcing. I've watched it happen with offshoring. The pattern is always the same: cut in a downturn, scramble to rebuild when the market turns, pay a premium to get back what you had.

AI is different in scale and speed. But the organisational mistake is identical.

The organisations that will win aren't the ones that cut the most. They're the ones that figured out which work AI actually does well, redesigned the roles around it, and kept the people who understood the domain well enough to supervise it.

Two questions worth sitting with if you're in a transformation role:
- Are you helping your organisation make that distinction? Or are you being asked to justify a headcount number that was decided before anyone modelled the work?
- If 30% of your AI-related headcount cuts come back as contractors in 2029, what does that do to your business case today?

The Goldman warning is real. The Gartner warning is also real. They're not contradictions — they're a sequence.

What's your read on how your industry is handling this?
