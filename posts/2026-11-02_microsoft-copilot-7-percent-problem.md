# Microsoft Copilot's 7% Problem

## Story/Angle
Microsoft's Q1 FY2027 earnings (reported 2 November 2026) showed Copilot daily users growing 5x year-over-year and cloud revenue up 30% to $63.9B. But PYMNTS buried the most important number: fewer than 7% of Microsoft's 450 million commercial seats are paid Copilot users. Microsoft is also shifting to consumption-based pricing and walking away from the consumer market entirely.

## LinkedIn Post

Microsoft just reported that daily Copilot users grew fivefold in a year.

The headline looks incredible. But bury yourself in the detail and a different picture emerges.

Microsoft has 450 million commercial seats. Fewer than 7% are paying for Copilot.

That means roughly 418 million seats — licences already inside organisations — aren't using the product Microsoft has bet its next decade on.

This isn't a product problem. Microsoft's cloud revenue hit $63.9 billion this quarter, up 30%. Companies with 50,000+ Copilot seats grew eightfold year-on-year. The product is working where it's being used.

The problem is rollout. Adoption. Change management. Call it whatever you like — the gap between "we have Copilot" and "our people actually use Copilot" is enormous, and most organisations are still sitting in it.

I've seen this pattern before. Same thing happened with Office 365 in 2015. Same thing with Teams in 2020. The technology arrives years before the organisation catches up.

Microsoft is now shifting to consumption-based pricing — you pay for what your agents actually do, not just for seats. That's smart. It removes the "we're paying for this and not using it" friction that kills adoption conversations. But it also raises the stakes. Every agent that fumbles a task now has a dollar amount attached to it.

Three questions every transformation leader should be asking right now:
1. What % of our Copilot seats are actively used each week?
2. Who owns adoption, and are they measured on it?
3. Do we know what "good" looks like for our most common use cases?

If you don't have answers to all three, you're in the 93%.

What's your organisation's honest Copilot adoption number?
