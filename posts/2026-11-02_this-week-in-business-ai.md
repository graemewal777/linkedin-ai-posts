# This Week in Business AI — 2 November 2026

## Story/Angle
Three stories defined this week: Microsoft's Q1 FY2027 earnings showing Copilot scaling faster than expected but penetration still thin; Goldman Sachs and Gartner clashing over the AI jobs outlook; and new research reframing where enterprise AI actually breaks down.

## LinkedIn Post

This week in business AI — here's what stood out:

✅ THE WIN
Microsoft reported Copilot daily users grew fivefold year-over-year, with its cloud business up 30% to $63.9B in a single quarter. Companies deploying 50,000+ seats grew eightfold. The kicker? Fewer than 7% of Microsoft's 450 million commercial seats are paid Copilot users. That's not a warning — that's a growth runway most SaaS companies would kill for.

⚠️ THE WARNING
New research analysing 10,000+ enterprise AI failure events found that less than 10% involved hallucinations. The real problem? 31% were escalation and resolution failures — agents that didn't know when to stop, hand off, or ask for help. We've spent two years building guardrails for the wrong risk.

📈 THE TREND
Goldman Sachs says AI could displace 1 in 11 workers — but argues companies are saving the big cuts for the next recession, when they'll have cover. Gartner flipped the script: 30% of workers laid off due to AI will be rehired by 2029, at significantly higher cost. The lesson isn't that AI won't change headcount. It's that organisations that slash first and ask questions later will pay for it.

---
The Copilot number that keeps me up at night isn't the fivefold growth. It's the 93% of seats sitting unused. Every one of those is either an untapped win or a boardroom story waiting to go sideways. The difference is whether anyone owns the rollout.
